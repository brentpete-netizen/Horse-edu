## Overview

The Shetland Pony is a hardy native pony of the Shetland Isles, Scotland. The Shetland Pony Stud-Book Society (founded 1890) holds the stud-book of origin and sets the world-wide breed standard for registered Shetlands. Registered adults must not exceed 42 inches (10.5 hands); ponies over 34 inches are Standard and those 34 inches and under are Miniatures. Today the breed is used mainly as a child’s riding pony and for carriage driving.

## Key characteristics

Maximum height 42 in (107 cm) at the withers. Any horse colour except spotted. Winter double coat with rain-shedding guard hairs; summer coat short with silky sheen; long, straight, profuse mane and tail. Small, proportional head; small erect ears; broad forehead; bold dark intelligent eyes; broad muzzle with open nostrils. Strong body with heart room, well-sprung ribs, muscular loin; broad long quarters with high-set tail; sloping shoulder and well-defined wither. Flat bone, springy pasterns, tough round feet; straight free action. Essential overall vitality, stamina, and robustness. Heavier draught-type and lighter riding-type lines both occur within the breed. Note: spotted/Appaloosa colour is excluded by the SPS-BS standard and is not listed among Notion color options as preferred.

## History

Small ponies have lived in the Shetland Isles for over 2,000 years (Bronze Age bones; long domestic use). Island isolation and harsh winters shaped hardiness and small size. Traditional work included peat and seaweed packing and transport; draught use grew after mid-19th-century roads. After the 1847 ban on children in coal pits, Shetland colts were widely used underground. Late 1800s exports surged (reports of over 1,000 a year), including to North America. The Shetland Pony Stud-Book Society was formed in 1890 (first native pony stud book in Britain; first volume 1891). The Marquis of Londonderry’s Bressay/Noss stud (1870s) strongly influenced modern type.
