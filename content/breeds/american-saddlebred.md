## Overview

The American Saddlebred is a show-oriented American breed known for commanding presence, animated gaits, and a patient temperament suited to riders of many levels. All Saddlebreds perform walk, trot, and canter; five-gaited horses also show the slow gait and rack.

## Key characteristics

Average height about 15–16 hands (often cited 15.1–16.3); weight about 1,000–1,200 lb. Well-proportioned with large expressive eyes, long arched neck, prominent withers, deep sloping shoulders, strong level back, high-set proudly carried tail. All Saddlebreds perform walk, trot, and canter; five-gaited horses also show the slow gait and rack. Movement should be fluid, animated, and balanced. Any color acceptable; chestnut, bay, brown, and black most common, with gray, roan, palomino, and pinto also seen.

## History

Evolved from Kentucky Saddlers in the 1800s from Thoroughbred and trotting horse lines crossed with pacers, Hackneys, Morgans, and related stock; roots include Narragansett Pacer influence. Used in the Civil War; show culture grew in Kentucky. Breed association founded 1891 in Louisville (first organization for an American horse breed); today ASHBA is based at the Kentucky Horse Park.
