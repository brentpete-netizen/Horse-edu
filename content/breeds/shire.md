## Overview

The Shire is England’s iconic heavy draft breed, known for great size, substance, and a docile expression. The Shire Horse Society (from 1878 as the English Cart Horse Society; “Shire” name from 1884) maintains the breed standard of points and stud book.

## Key characteristics

Minimum mature height: stallions 17 hh (173 cm), mares 16 hh (163 cm), geldings 16.2 hh (168 cm); stallions often average ~17.2 hh. Stallion colours black, brown, bay or grey (not roan/chestnut; large body white patches undesirable). Mares may also be roan (not a Notion color option). Long lean head, slightly Roman nose; deep oblique shoulder; short strong back; substantial flat bone (~11 in stallions); deep wide feet; fine straight silky feather (modern preference for cleaner legs). Straight, forceful action.

## History

English heavy cart horse whose numbers grew with 19th-century local haulage needs. English Cart Horse Society founded 1878 (encouraged by future King Edward VII); first Spring Show 1880 at Islington. Name “Shire” and Society title adopted 1884. Major exporter by late 1880s (e.g. 1,400 pedigree export certificates in 1888). Mechanisation and post-WWII decline left very few registrations by 1958; dedicated breeders kept the Society going. Today the Shire Horse Society promotes the breed, maintains the stud book, and runs the National Spring Show.
