## Overview

The Welsh Pony and Cob (native to Wales; Notion Origin = Other) comprises four purebred Stud Book sections (A–D) plus Part-Breds, ranging from the small Welsh Mountain Pony to the larger Welsh Cob. Hardy native Welsh stock, they are used for riding, driving, and many performance sports.

## Key characteristics

Section A (Welsh Mountain Pony) not exceeding 12.0 hh (121.9 cm); Section B (Welsh Pony) and Section C (Welsh Pony of Cob Type) not exceeding 13.2 hh (137.2 cm); Section D (Welsh Cob) exceeds 13.2 hh with no upper limit. Cob-type (C/D): strong, hardy, active, pony character with substance; quality head (coarse/Roman nose objectionable); free, true, forcible action. Colour: any except piebald and skewbald.

## History

Welsh Cob recognised as a breed by the 15th century per early Welsh literature; used for draft and family transport. Welsh Pony & Cob Society Stud Book began 1901; Section D height rules refined over time (by 1950 minimum 13.2 hh, no upper limit). Today valued as versatile family and performance equines.
