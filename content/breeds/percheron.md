## Overview

The Percheron is a French draft breed from Le Perche, known for a proud, willing disposition, clean-legged quality, and typically black or grey colour. Widely used historically for farm and city work in America; today also for hitching, recreation, and riding.

## Key characteristics

Most mature horses about 16–18+ hands (often 16.2–17.3); weight commonly around 1,900 lb (up to ~2,300–2,600). Usually black or grey; sorrel/bay/roan also occur; moderate white markings acceptable, excessive white undesirable. Heavy lower-thigh muscling; clean action; long level croup, deep wide chest; attractive head and arched neck. PHAOA materials note a traditional association with Arabian ancestry in breed lore.

## History

Originated in Le Perche, France (southwest of Paris). PHAOA notes it is widely believed Arabian blood influenced early development; 17th-century Percherons (~15–16 hh) pulled French mail coaches; French government stud at Le Pin developed army mounts. All modern lines trace to Jean Le Blanc (foaled 1823). U.S.: Edward Harris’s 1839 import (Diligence the sole survivor of four); Normandy and Louis Napoleon to Ohio in 1851. Massive late-19th-century imports from Le Perche; by 1930 U.S. census showed about three times as many registered Percherons as the other four draft breeds combined. Near collapse after tractor mechanisation; preserved by dedicated farmers including many Amish. Registry lineage: Norman-Percheron Association / first U.S. purebred livestock association (Chicago, 1876); Percheron Society of America (1905); Percheron Horse Association of America (1934, nonprofit). At peak, association registered over 10,000 head/year.
