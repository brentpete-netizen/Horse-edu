## Overview

The Irish Draught is a versatile, powerful, athletic Irish foundation breed with substance and quality, known for a willing temperament and sound constitution. Historically a farm, hunt, and driving horse; today also valued purebred and as a base for performance crosses. Horse Sport Ireland keeps the studbook of origin.

## Key characteristics

Ideal mature height 15.2–16.3 hh (158–170 cm); about 23 cm (9 in) clean flat bone. Pleasant head (not coarse), wide forehead, kind eyes; good length of rein; sloping shoulder; deep girth, strong back, loins, and hindquarters; strong gaskins and clean hocks; straight free active movement. Strong whole colours (bay, grey, chestnut, black, brown, dun); excessive white undesirable.

## History

Developed as a working farm horse in Ireland, with references dating to the 18th century; used for farm work alongside riding, hunting, and driving. Foundation breed for many leisure and performance crosses (including Irish Sport Horse production). Irish Draught Horse Studbook established 1971 (originally Bord na gCapall / Irish Horse Register); Horse Sport Ireland now operates the Studbook of Origin and breeding programme. Designated rare breed by Ireland’s Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine. Official daughter/filial studbooks: IDHSGB, IDHSC (Canada), IDHSNA (U.S., society founded 1993). Modern purebred uses include Ireland’s Garda Síochána Mounted Unit (exclusively Irish Draughts per HSI). Influential jumping sires cited by HSI include King of Diamonds and Clover Hill.
