## Overview

The Standardbred is North America’s harness-racing breed, racing as trotters or pacers while pulling a sulky. Known for a willing temperament and athleticism; many go on to second careers after racing. The U.S. Trotting Association (founded 1939) is the breed registry.

## Key characteristics

Most stand about 15–16 hands. Weight commonly cited about 800–1,200 lb on USTA fan materials and about 950–1,200 lb in USTA life-after-racing materials—use as approximate ranges. Athletic, slender build with stronger bone than a typical Thoroughbred; bay and brown most common. Competes as a trotter (diagonal gait) or pacer (lateral gait) in harness. Gentle, trainable disposition valued for life after racing.

## History

Breed beginnings linked to the Thoroughbred Messenger (imported to the U.S. in the 18th century); virtually every North American Standardbred traces to Messenger’s great-grandson Hambletonian 10 (foaled 5 May 1849, Sugar Loaf, N.Y.). The name reflects early registration requiring a standard mile time. Informal road and fair racing grew from the early 1800s; purpose-built tracks from the mid-1800s. USTA founded 1939 as the Standardbred registry and administrative body for harness racing; sole issuer of registration documents required for North American racing and breeding; HQ in Columbus/Westerville, Ohio area since 1948. The Hambletonian stakes (since 1926; Meadowlands since 1981) is named for the foundation sire.
