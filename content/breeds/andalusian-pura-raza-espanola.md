## Overview

The Pura Raza Española (PRE), often called the Andalusian, is Spain’s purebred Spanish horse: a harmonious, beautiful baroque riding horse with elevated elastic gaits and a noble, trainable temperament. ANCCE maintains the PRE studbook and racial prototype.

## Key characteristics

Eumetric, mesolinear, subconvex frontal profile; males 1.54–1.72 m at withers, females 1.52–1.70 m (below minimum disqualifies; above ~1.70/1.72 penalized). Proportioned head with expressive eyes; mobile ears; elevated, elastic, cadence-rich gaits with aptitude for collection and turns on the haunches. Temperament described as spirited, noble, docile, and balanced with strong learning ability. Extensive white markings can be disqualifying under PRE rules.

## History

PRE breeding developed over centuries toward a beautiful, agile riding horse distinct from heavier medieval types. Landmark stresses include the 19th-century French invasion and the Industrial Revolution’s impact on agricultural roles; a royal register for the Spanish Horse was created under King Alphonse III (per ANCCE). Spain’s Civil War disrupted breeding; recovery followed. Breeders’ association founded 1972 and later became national ANCCE; Spain transferred PRE studbook management to ANCCE in 2007 (per IALHA studbook history). African Horse Sickness outbreak (1991) helped spur SICAB, now a major international PRE fair. In North America, IALHA (merged 1995 from earlier U.S. Andalusian registries dating to the 1960s) maintains a studbook/registry for PRE and Lusitano horses based on mother-country studbooks—distinct from ANCCE’s Spanish studbook of origin.
