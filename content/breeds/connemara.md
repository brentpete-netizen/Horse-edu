## Overview

The Connemara is Ireland’s native pony breed: a compact, well-balanced riding pony valued for hardiness, surefootedness, intelligence, and athletic/jumping ability. The Connemara Pony Breeders’ Society (CPBS), founded 1923, holds the stud book of origin (first volume 1926).

## Key characteristics

Adult height 128–148 cm (about 12.2–14.2 hh) at five years without shoes; over-height class above 148 cm. Compact with depth, substance, and heart room on short legs; medium-length head with large kindly eyes; sloping shoulders; deep body, strong back and loins; flat bone 18–21 cm; free, easy, true movement without undue knee action. CPBS breeding aims include jumping and athletic ability. Colours include black, bay, brown, chestnut, dun/buckskin, palomino, cream dilutes, roans, and grey.

## History

Native to Connemara in western Ireland. Congested Districts Board (from 1891) earlier introduced outside stallions with mixed results. CPBS founded 1923 to preserve and improve the breed from carefully selected native stock; first studbook published 1926 (93 mares, 9 stallions). Foundation male lines include Cannon Ball/Rebel (Blue), Connemara Boy (Red), and later Mountain Lad (Green); later introductions included Irish Draught and Thoroughbred blood (e.g. Carna Dun/Purple Line) and Clonkeehan Auratum (Orange Line, 1950s). CPBS breeding programme continues Class inspections to the published breed standard.
