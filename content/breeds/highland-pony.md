## Overview

The Highland Pony is one of two native pony breeds of the Scottish Highlands and Islands (alongside the Eriskay): a strong, well-balanced, compact pony with substance, suited to ride, drive, and pack work across many disciplines.

## Key characteristics

Height not to exceed 14.2 hh (148 cm). Well-carried alert head with kindly eye; sloping shoulder; compact body with well-sprung ribs; powerful quarters and clean flat hocks; flat hard bone, short cannons, broad dark hoofs; soft silky feather; natural flowing mane and full tail. Straight free action without undue knee action. Dense winter double coat (strong outer hair over soft undercoat) shed in spring. Colours: range of duns (often with dorsal stripe/zebra markings), grey, brown, black, bay, occasionally liver chestnut with silver mane/tail. Broken colours not allowed; white markings discouraged (small star only).

## History

One of two native pony breeds of the Scottish Highlands and Islands, adapted over centuries to harsh climate (dense winter double coat). Traditionally the crofter’s draught pony for peat, seaweed, ploughing, and cart work; later widely used for riding, showing, endurance, estate/hill work, and conservation grazing. Highland Pony Society founded 1923 (centenary 2023); stud-book records extend from 1894. Society maintains the breed description and stallion licensing (max 14.2 hh; strict white-marking limits for stallions).
